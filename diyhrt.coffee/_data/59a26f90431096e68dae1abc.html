// what this site is

we do

  • check a curated set of vendors around the clock and show what's in stock
  • ping you when something you want comes back
  • point at each vendor's lab testing, canary and reviews

we don't

  • sell anything, hold stock or take payments. links go straight to the vendor
  • give medical advice. dosing, bloodwork, side effects: see diy hrt 101 below
  • call any vendor safe or approved. we show what was published, when, by whom

similar projects: valerie.vg (community sourcing information) and the older hrtcafe (down at the time of writing). same general idea, our own checks and servers.

// diy hrt 101 (not here)

this site deliberately doesn't host medical information; others do it far better. start with these:

  • diyhrt.infocommunity knowledge base, successor to diyhrt.wiki
  • trans.diyharm-reduction database for self-administered hrt
  • transfemsciencepharmacology, dosing references, reading bloodwork
  • a practical guide to feminising hrtby Katie Tightpussy
  • r/TransDIYvendor reviews, advice, troubleshooting
  • trans harm reductionindependent lab testing and a results database

things we will say out loud

  • get bloods done. diy or not, your levels matter more than your dose schedule.
  • check lab results yourself. janoshik and trans harm reduction both let you confirm a report with its task number and key.
  • start low. the sources above cover what the community considers sensible starting doses.
  • read more than one source. information online is a snapshot, and diy changes fast.
// helping out

spotted a vendor we should track, a missing lab result, or a bug? use the contact form or the discord, which is quicker and where things like funding a lab test get organised.

a new vendor isn't tracked until there's published lab testing we can point at. until then they show
testing · in progress
with no stock checks. to help pay for a test, trans harm reduction and artemis analytical both take community-funded submissions.

canaries: this site has its own at /canary, with step-by-step checking. a vendor canary shows up as a chip on their card.
